04 — Rear Window Replacement

Back glass, done properly.

Rear windows are tempered glass, so when they break, they break completely. Replacing one is more than dropping in a new pane: the defroster grid, radio or GPS antenna, and on many cars the high-mount brake light all connect through the glass. We fit the correct part for your vehicle, bond it with urethane like a windshield, and reconnect and test every circuit before you leave.

How it works

Three steps, one visit.

STEP 1

Identify the glass

Rear glass varies by body style, tint, wiper and antenna options. Your VIN lets us order the exact part.

STEP 2

Remove and clean

We clear the remaining glass, remove the trim and old urethane, and vacuum the cabin and cargo area.

STEP 3

Bond and reconnect

The new glass is set with urethane, the electrical tabs are reconnected, and the defroster is tested before you drive off.

Hatchbacks, SUVs and trucks

On a hatchback or SUV the rear glass is part of the liftgate and often carries a wiper motor and the third brake light; on a pickup the back glass may be a fixed piece or a sliding unit. We handle all of them. If a slider’s latch or track is broken we can usually replace the whole assembly.

Drive-away time

Because rear glass is urethane-bonded, plan on the same 30–60 minute safe-drive-away time as a windshield. Leave the retention tape on for a day and skip the car wash for 24 hours so the seal cures undisturbed.
